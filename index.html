Is this decision a good fit for the business?

Different products need different supply-chain responses. Standard power drills often rely on make-to-stock, while fitted kitchens are typically make-to-order.

Make to stock (MTS)

Finished products are produced before the customer order. This supports fast availability but creates forecast and inventory risk.

Make to order (MTO)

Production begins after the customer order and specifications are known. This supports customization but usually requires a longer lead time.

Key takeaway

Where you decouple shapes speed, customization, and risk

The decoupling point defines what is made to stock versus what is made to order. Moving it downstream usually improves delivery speed. Moving it upstream usually improves customization.

  • Make to stock

    Place the decoupling point closer to the customer when fast delivery matters and finished products can be standardized.

  • Make to order

    Place the decoupling point earlier in the process when customer requirements must guide what gets produced.

  • Trade-off

    Faster delivery often increases forecast and inventory risk, while more customization usually increases lead-time risk.

Remember: there is no single best decoupling point for every product. The right choice depends on how much speed, customization, and risk the business can accept.
